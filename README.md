# Vendor Spend Strategy Memo: project folder

Acquisition due-diligence review of 386 vendors ($7,887,359 of last-12-month spend) for an acquired software company, done end to end in the **Claude Code CLI**. Nothing was typed into the spreadsheet by hand.

**Result:** $1,764,587 of estimated annual savings (22.4% of vendor spend), of which $1,420,107 comes from three opportunities. Excluding $226,932 of deal and prior-owner fees that end anyway, the total is $1,537,655.

| # | Opportunity | Est. annual savings |
|---|---|---:|
| 1 | Salesforce CRM contract reset | $467,584 |
| 2 | Move back-office finance, legal and hiring vendors to the parent company | $530,901 |
| 3 | Shrink the office footprint from seven sites to three | $421,622 |

## Reproduce

```
python3 build.py && python3 qc.py && python3 make_workbook.py
```

`build.py` and `qc.py` use only the Python standard library. `make_workbook.py` needs `openpyxl` for the `.xlsx`, and renders `executive_memo.pdf` with headless Chrome when it is installed (it stops if the memo is not exactly one page).

## How it was done

1. **Read the source.** Claude Code read the exported CSV and kept an untouched copy as the input.
2. **Set department rules.** Each vendor is classified by its primary function, using five ordered rules (see Departments below and the comment block in `build.py`).
3. **Classified every vendor** with a department from the sheet's Config tab, a one-line description, an action and a written rationale. Vendors in the same situation share a rationale template (defined in `build.py`) so they are treated the same way.
4. **Verified unknowns by web search.** 24 searches on the larger vendors that could not be identified from the name. Examples resolved: Cloudcrossing (maker of PDF Butler), GPT Space&Co, HRsolution International, 4i Advisory, Harmonic Group, New Star Networks, Veniture, TP Prime, Limes Plus, Calm Achiever, Westbrook Advisers. Vendors that still could not be confirmed are marked Low confidence instead of being given an invented description.
5. **Applied the acquisition lens.** Deal advisers and prior-owner fees terminate; back-office vendors consolidate into the parent company; satellite offices close.
6. **Quantified savings per vendor**, then rolled them up. The Top 3 and the memo are generated by script: every dollar figure and count is computed from the vendor rows. Wording such as site counts and assumed rates is written by hand.
7. **Quality-checked four ways**: automated checks, an independent AI review of recommendations and savings, a blind re-classification of every department, and a final independent review of the whole submission against the brief.

### Recommendation rules

| Action | Applied when |
|---|---|
| **Terminate** | No ongoing need: deal advisers and prior-owner fees, recruitment agencies, satellite offices, one-off meals, events and gifts, completed projects, tools made redundant by one already licensed, individuals paid as vendors, and unidentified payees under $5K. |
| **Consolidate** | Two or more vendors serve the same function, a supplier has duplicate records, or spend bypasses an existing preferred channel (for example hotels booked outside Navan). |
| **Optimize** | The vendor is needed and has no duplicate; the lever is price, licence count or usage. Also used for statutory charges and for unidentified vendors of $5K or more, which must be verified before any action. |

### Savings model

Estimated saving = last-12-month cost × a rate. Defaults: Terminate 100%, Consolidate 30%, Optimize 10%. Exceptions are listed with reasons in `build.py` (`TEMPLATE_RATE`, `VENDOR_RATE`). The main ones:

| Rate | Applies to | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 0% | Statutory and government charges, duplicate-record merges, individual payees, unidentified and unverified vendors, pass-through benefits and student labour | Nothing is assumed where the spend cannot be reduced or has not been verified |
| 10% | Insurance brokers, employer of record | Spend is mostly pass-through premiums or salaries; saving on the fee only |
| 15% | Salesforce; health insurers | Salesforce: range 10-20% until the contract type and terms are reviewed. Health: pooled pricing, cover continues |
| 20% | Audit, tax and UK legal consolidation | The work is still required in each country |
| 40-50% | London, Zagreb and India offices | Space resized to hybrid attendance |
| 50% | One-off meals, events, gifts, tail purchases | Half is assumed to reappear on expenses |
| 70% | Recruitment agencies, Australia and Singapore coworking | Some agency hiring and on-demand desks will still be needed |

### Departments

From the Config tab: Engineering, Facilities, G&A, Legal, M&A, Marketing, SaaS, Product, Professional Services, Sales, Support, Finance.

A vendor is classified by its **primary function**: the department that normally buys and owns that kind of vendor. A theory about how this particular company uses it may break a tie but never override the function. First rule that fits:

1. Deal and ownership costs (advisers, data rooms, owner fees) go to M&A.
2. A tool or service that serves one function goes to that function: CRM, sales intelligence and customer procurement portals to Sales; marketing automation, PR, conferences, merchandise and print to Marketing; cloud, developer tools and outsourced development to Engineering; product management and design to Product; accounting, audit, tax, payroll processing, banking and insurance broking to Finance (employer-of-record and staffing are people costs and go to G&A); law firms, entity management, IP and escrow to Legal; PSA and certification delivery to Professional Services; helpdesk tooling to Support.
3. Company-wide business software with no single owner goes to SaaS.
4. Physical workplace (offices, utilities, telecoms, supplies, pantry, couriers) goes to Facilities.
5. Everything else (benefits, recruiting, travel, meals, events, IT hardware, individuals, unidentified payees) goes to G&A.

No vendor fitted Support.

**Correction made during review.** The first pass classified Salesforce as Engineering. I had inferred from its size and the Salesforce-related vendors around it that the product was built on Salesforce, and let that theory override what the vendor is: a CRM, which is a Sales tool. The first AI review missed it because the reviewer was given the theory as context. The fix was to the process: the rules above were written down, and all 386 vendors were re-run through a blind classifier that saw none of my departments or assumptions. It agreed on 370 of 386 (95.9%); 6 departments changed in total and 12 disagreements were kept with a recorded reason. Full log: `output/department_recheck.md`.

## Quality checks

**Automated (`qc.py`, results in `output/qc_report.md`): 15 of 15 pass.** They cover: every source vendor present in the original order; costs unchanged and total reconciled to $7,887,359; no blanks; departments and actions within the allowed lists; descriptions one line, at most 160 characters, no vague filler; each saving between $0 and the vendor's cost; statutory charges at $0; savings totals reconciled; Top 3 equal to the sum of their members with no vendor counted twice; duplicate supplier records flagged. The fifteenth check confirms that all 27 Consolidate rows with a rationale unique to one vendor were read by hand (`data/manual_review.txt`).

**Alignment guard.** Each classification line carries a vendor-name key. `build.py` stops without writing anything if a key does not match its source row, so a description cannot slide onto the wrong vendor.

**Independent AI review.** A separate Claude Code sub-agent, given the output CSV, my business-context assumptions and a checklist, read all 386 rows (top 100 by spend closely). It returned 29 findings; 71 of the top 100 had no issue. 26 were applied and 3 were judgment calls left as they were. The review made the savings more conservative: the total fell from $2,276,944 to $1,801,526 at that point. The log is in `output/qc_ai_review_findings.md`.

Reviewer prompt: `prompts/subagent_1_independent_review.md`.

**Blind department re-check.** See the correction note under Departments. The classifier prompt (`prompts/subagent_2_blind_department_classifier.md`) gave only vendor names, neutral descriptions and the five rules.

**Final independent review.** A third sub-agent was given the assignment brief and grading criteria but none of my reasoning, and graded every deliverable (prompt: `prompts/subagent_3_final_review.md`). It recomputed all figures from the CSV and found them consistent. It returned 35 findings: 26 were applied, 3 partly applied, 5 left unchanged with a recorded reason, and 1 is the re-import of the final workbook into the live sheet. Main changes: the Salesforce saving now states that the contract type must be confirmed first; like vendors carry the same rate (brokers, insurers, employer of record); statutory rows were removed from Opportunity 2; savings are also shown excluding fees that end anyway. Total savings fell from $1,801,526 to $1,764,587. Log: `output/qc_final_review.md`.

## Limitations

- **No contracts, invoices or usage data.** Everything is inferred from vendor names and 12 months of spend. Savings are estimates to confirm against contracts; exit costs are not netted off.
- **Confidence.** 278 vendors are High, 55 Medium, 53 Low. Low rows total $324,574 (4.1% of spend) and carry $5,240 of assumed saving. 37 of them are payees under $5K that could not be identified from the name and were not researched. The two that matter are Weking d.o.o. ($144,093) and Big Frontier / Cult of Monday ($66,131). Medium includes large rows whose saving depends on terms not yet seen, such as Salesforce and the office leases.
- **$226,932 of Opportunity 2** is deal-adviser and prior-owner cost that ends on its own (that these are prior-owner fees is inferred from the vendor names). It lowers the run rate but is not a negotiated saving.
- **Salesforce contract type is unknown.** $3.1M is large for internal CRM seats, and several vendors on the list are Salesforce-related, so it may be a platform or reseller agreement. The 15% estimate is kept under either route but the lever differs.
- **Office reductions (50% London, 40% Zagreb and India) are assumptions**, not measured attendance. Weking d.o.o. may be an eighth site and is not in Opportunity 3.
- **Scale.** The brief asks for savings that move the needle for a $1 billion business. This vendor list totals $7.9M, so the savings identified are about 22% of the spend in scope; I have not inflated them to fit.
- **Vendor names were modified by the assessment** and several have character-encoding damage (for example `SveuäIliå¡Te`). Names and costs are passed through untouched.
- **Web verification covered only the larger unknowns**, not all 386 vendors.
